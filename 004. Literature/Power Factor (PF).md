Power factor (PF) is ==a measure of electrical energy efficiency in AC circuits, defined as the ratio of real power (used for work) to apparent power (total power supplied)==. It indicates how effectively incoming power is converted to useful work, with a value of 1 (or 100%) being ideal and lower values signifying wasted power. In simpler terms, imagine it as a beer: the beer is the useful work (real power), the foam is the reactive power, and the entire glass is the apparent power.

- **[Real Power](https://www.google.com/search?newwindow=1&client=firefox-b-d&sca_esv=bcefe35088a0bd87&sxsrf=AE3TifObzuoHRnv3Lq1TUwjhVk-R-shwrw%3A1759046342003&q=Real+Power&sa=X&ved=2ahUKEwj17O6i_vqPAxVMkq8BHbr5H2kQxccNegQIJBAB&mstk=AUtExfAYEJGBI60umn9F4WKFCWlAspD-rB_HAzfz3IJuIT-8shm_RVDXWZkIWbE_ctv8vdKmWaMTD9Bs9btuu19jjSJP2gtgpUbAjAvK3MeKkA2M_kkqJzIPKdU9o1pzoAwvVsp-QODXC-0ZUiWVr6OMCkQEOz3LmzlhwK-iWAXMIItJZ6u_APz-R9nEZPrkGKXVcWqaezfwfGsSDkkeOnprSfnIwoRMT-ExNvKVKL3EwscahmSDGJMMjdVZ4_u1rq1rZjwsPXGbI50J71s380HOP3Y4&csui=3) (kW):** The power that actually performs work, such as heating or mechanical motion. 
- **[Reactive Power](https://www.google.com/search?newwindow=1&client=firefox-b-d&sca_esv=bcefe35088a0bd87&sxsrf=AE3TifObzuoHRnv3Lq1TUwjhVk-R-shwrw%3A1759046342003&q=Reactive+Power&sa=X&ved=2ahUKEwj17O6i_vqPAxVMkq8BHbr5H2kQxccNegUImAEQAQ&mstk=AUtExfAYEJGBI60umn9F4WKFCWlAspD-rB_HAzfz3IJuIT-8shm_RVDXWZkIWbE_ctv8vdKmWaMTD9Bs9btuu19jjSJP2gtgpUbAjAvK3MeKkA2M_kkqJzIPKdU9o1pzoAwvVsp-QODXC-0ZUiWVr6OMCkQEOz3LmzlhwK-iWAXMIItJZ6u_APz-R9nEZPrkGKXVcWqaezfwfGsSDkkeOnprSfnIwoRMT-ExNvKVKL3EwscahmSDGJMMjdVZ4_u1rq1rZjwsPXGbI50J71s380HOP3Y4&csui=3) (kVAr):** The power needed to create magnetic or electric fields, but which doesn't do useful work, like with motors or transformers. 
- **[Apparent Power](https://www.google.com/search?newwindow=1&client=firefox-b-d&sca_esv=bcefe35088a0bd87&sxsrf=AE3TifObzuoHRnv3Lq1TUwjhVk-R-shwrw%3A1759046342003&q=Apparent+Power&sa=X&ved=2ahUKEwj17O6i_vqPAxVMkq8BHbr5H2kQxccNegUInQEQAQ&mstk=AUtExfAYEJGBI60umn9F4WKFCWlAspD-rB_HAzfz3IJuIT-8shm_RVDXWZkIWbE_ctv8vdKmWaMTD9Bs9btuu19jjSJP2gtgpUbAjAvK3MeKkA2M_kkqJzIPKdU9o1pzoAwvVsp-QODXC-0ZUiWVr6OMCkQEOz3LmzlhwK-iWAXMIItJZ6u_APz-R9nEZPrkGKXVcWqaezfwfGsSDkkeOnprSfnIwoRMT-ExNvKVKL3EwscahmSDGJMMjdVZ4_u1rq1rZjwsPXGbI50J71s380HOP3Y4&csui=3) (kVA):** The total power, which is a combination of real and reactive power. 
The Power Factor Equation
The power factor is calculated by dividing the real power by the apparent power: 
PF = Real Power (kW) / Apparent Power (kVA) 
Why is Power Factor Important?
- **Efficiency:**
    A low power factor means a lot of apparent power is wasted as reactive power, making your system inefficient. 
- **Cost:**
    Wasted power means you're drawing more current from the utility than you need for the work done, which can lead to higher energy costs. 
- **Capacity:**
    A low power factor requires more current, which can overload electrical equipment and transmission lines. 
- **Voltage Fluctuations:**
    Reactive power can cause voltage to drop, impacting the quality and stability of your electrical supply. 
How to Improve Power Factor 
- **Install capacitors:** Capacitors can offset the reactive power drawn by inductive loads, improving the overall power factor and bringing it closer to 1.
